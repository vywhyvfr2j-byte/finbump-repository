App launch checklist Your bi-weekly app launch checklist: 30 steps from define to after launch. FinBump App launch checklist Next steps drawn from the patterns shared by well-rated, well-downloaded apps. Assumes a new app in planning, launching on iOS and Android, with a freemium subscription model and a small team. Skip or reword any task that doesn't fit. 1 of 30 done  ·  Progress saves to this page Define  ·  Design  ·  Build  ·  Earn & retain  ·  Store & launch  ·  After launch Phase 1  ·  Define the core  ·  1/4 Decide what the app is for before deciding what it contains. ☐ Write the one-sentence description of the app's core job
If it takes more than one sentence, the scope is still too wide. ☐ Choose 3 to 5 launch features that directly serve the core job
Put every other idea on a “later” list instead of the roadmap. ☐ Pick the monetization model
Default assumed here: freemium with a subscription and a 7-day free trial. ☐ Set target numbers for success
Onboarding completion, Day 1 / Day 7 / Day 30 retention, trial-to-paid conversion, and a 4.5+ store rating. Phase 2  ·  Design structure and onboarding  ·  0/7 The first session decides whether people come back. ☐ Map navigation to 3 to 5 main tabs
Move everything else into a profile or More area. ☐ Check that key actions take no more than 2 to 3 taps from home
Walk through each launch feature and count. ☐ Design a first session that delivers value within about a minute
Let people try the core action before asking them to sign up. ☐ Limit onboarding to 3 to 5 screens
Each screen should personalize the app or explain something non-obvious. ☐ Plan permission requests in context
Ask for notifications, location or camera only when the feature needs it, with a short explainer first. ☐ Add frictionless sign-in
Sign in with Apple, Google sign-in, and a guest mode where it makes sense. ☐ Follow platform conventions and accessibility basics
Apple HIG and Material Design patterns, dark mode, dynamic text size, screen-reader labels. Phase 3  ·  Build the quality foundation  ·  0/5 Slowness and crashes are the most common reasons for one-star reviews. ☐ Set performance targets
Cold start under about 2 seconds and crash-free sessions at 99% or higher. ☐ Install crash reporting and analytics before beta
For example Firebase Crashlytics or Sentry, plus a product analytics tool. ☐ Track the events behind your targets
Onboarding steps, the core action, paywall views, trial starts and purchases. ☐ Make the core flow work offline or on a weak connection
At minimum, show saved content and a clear message instead of a blank screen. ☐ Cover privacy requirements
Fill in the App Store privacy labels and Play data safety form, collect only what you need, and add in-app account deletion. Phase 4  ·  Monetization and retention  ·  0/5 A fair, clearly explained price converts better than a hidden one. ☐ Build the paywall and place it well
Show it at the end of onboarding and whenever someone taps a premium feature. ☐ Make pricing and trial terms plain
State the price, trial length and renewal, and include Restore Purchases and how to cancel. ☐ Use platform billing
StoreKit on iOS and Google Play Billing on Android for digital purchases. ☐ Add retention mechanics that fit the core job
Progress tracking, streaks, saved history or reminders, whichever matches what users come back for. ☐ Write a notification plan
Opt-in only, tied to something useful, with a frequency cap. Phase 5  ·  Store listing and launch prep  ·  0/5 The listing is the first screen most people ever see. ☐ Design the icon and benefit-led screenshots
Each screenshot should say what the user gets, not just show a screen. ☐ Write keyword-aware title, subtitle and description
Put the core value in the first lines of the description. ☐ Add in-app feedback and a help page
Give unhappy users a way to reach you before they leave a review. ☐ Trigger the rating prompt after a positive moment
Use the native prompt (on iOS it can appear at most 3 times a year). ☐ Run a beta and fix the top issues
TestFlight on iOS and Play internal or closed testing on Android. Phase 6  ·  After launch  ·  0/4 Successful apps improve in small, frequent steps. ☐ Review metrics against your targets every week
Start with onboarding completion and Day 1 retention. ☐ Respond to store reviews, starting with low ratings
Thank people, explain fixes, and note recurring complaints. ☐ Ship small updates every 2 to 4 weeks
Assumed cadence for a small team; adjust to your capacity. ☐ Add features only when data shows demand
Each new feature should strengthen the core job, not compete with it. Open the FinBump app to update your progress on each step. Open FinBump You receive this reminder every other Wednesday. It is sent to the admin account on FinBump.
